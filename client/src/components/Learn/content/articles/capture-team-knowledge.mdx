## Why it matters

Your team already knows how to do the work. How to frame the analysis. Which template holds up under pressure. What a client in this sector always pushes back on. Most of that knowledge lives in someone’s head, and it leaves when they go on leave or move projects.

Every new person has to ask or guess, and every new chat starts from zero.

MyAssist gives your team somewhere to put that knowledge. A prompt saved once stops someone rewriting it next week. A proven approach captured as a skill lets the newest person start where the most experienced one left off. Everyone then works from the same shared starting point.

## Turn a SharePoint page into a skill

Start with a ways-of-working page your team already keeps, such as how you run a client kickoff.

<Steps>

1. Export the page as a document and attach it to a new chat. If the SharePoint connector is available to you, you can use that instead.
2. Ask MyAssist to draft a SKILL.md with the Skill Builder skill. See [the ways to create a skill](/learn/articles/guides/skills#choose-a-creation-route).
3. Review the draft. Remove steps that are out of date and add what the page assumes people already know.
4. Test the skill with a real request, including an incomplete one.
5. Share it with your team.

</Steps>

## Hold a weekly skills review

Meet weekly. Each person brings a skill they’re working on or trying out.

Use the meeting to answer three questions:

- What works?
- What doesn’t?
- What can the team chronicle together?

Anything that works moves into a shared skill. Anything that doesn’t gets reworked or retired.

## Find something your team regularly repeats

Spend 15 minutes listing the repeatable tasks your team performs:

- Tasks you’ve explained to more than two people
- Templates people copy and edit
- Review comments that keep coming back
- Prompts people write the same way each week

Your [prompt log](/learn/articles/write-an-effective-prompt#keep-a-prompt-log) is a good place to spot these.

## Keep it alive

A shared skill goes stale unless someone looks after it. Name an owner for each one, and review it in your weekly meeting.
