## Why this matters

A prompt is the instruction, question or request you give MyAssist. The clearer it is, the less MyAssist has to guess and the more useful the response.

You don’t need a perfect formula. You need enough direction to remove guesswork.

## The GCSE method

GCSE stands for Goal, Context, Source and Expectations. Each part adds information that makes the response more accurate and relevant.

- **Goal**: what outcome do you want?
- **Context**: what background should MyAssist know?
- **Source**: what information should MyAssist use?
- **Expectations**: what format, style or level of detail do you want?

<Callout type="analogy">

Build your prompt like a layered cake. Each GCSE part adds something, and together they give MyAssist a clearer brief.

</Callout>

## Before and after GCSE

The same request gets clearer and more specific with each GCSE part.

<Compare>
  <Before>Summarise this report.</Before>
  <After>

- **Goal**: Help me prepare for a client meeting.
- **Context**: I’m meeting the client’s CFO to discuss the findings.
- **Source**: Use the attached report only.
- **Expectations**: Create a one-page executive summary covering the key findings, risks, opportunities and three questions I should ask.

</After>
</Compare>

If the response misses the mark, change one GCSE part and try again. Add context, name a source or clarify the format you need.

## Keep a prompt log

Keep a running note of the prompts you write during a conversation, in a notepad or a document. After a few days, read it back and look for repeats.

Patterns show you what to save. An instruction you retype often belongs in a saved prompt. A whole way of working belongs in a skill. If your team writes the same prompts, see [find what your team repeats](/learn/articles/capture-team-knowledge#find-what-your-team-repeats).
